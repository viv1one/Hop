# Brag Plan: HOP

## What is this app?
HOP is a serverless, no-accounts, proximity-first mesh app: short photos and videos and end-to-end encrypted messages travel phone-to-phone over Bluetooth LE + WiFi Direct — and, for wider reach, across a peer-to-peer DHT — with no company server anywhere in the loop, ever.

## The angle
The README says it outright, and it's the whole pitch: "There is no HOP-operated backend that could be subpoenaed, breached, sold, or quietly start harvesting data — because it doesn't exist." Lead with that as a stated fact, not a feature bullet. The video's job is to make the absence of a server feel like the most aggressive design decision in the video, not an apology for missing infrastructure.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
Black frame. Giant white type slams in: "THERE IS NO SERVER." Beat. Smaller line drops in underneath: "Because we never built one."

## Key moments (the middle)
- The reach-tier picker: Locality → Town → City → Country, each pill arriving in turn, one settling as selected — the one piece of "mesh" the user ever actually touches.
- A phone-to-phone connection line drawing between two device silhouettes, then pulling back to reveal it's one edge in a much larger mesh of nodes — the local hop expanding into the DHT.
- A lock snapping shut over a message bubble, with a line making the specific claim: not even the relay can read it.

## Outro / punchline
Return to black. The README's own line, delivered slowly, one clause at a time: "No server to subpoena. No server to breach. No server to sell." Beat. Final line: "Because it doesn't exist." Wordmark holds: HOP. Tagline: "Serverless. Yours."

## User flow worth showing
Pick a photo → choose a reach tier (Locality → Country) → it spreads phone-to-phone, then peer-to-peer for wider tiers → open a chat with no phone number, encrypted end to end. Entry → key action (tier pick + post) → result (it reaches someone, privately).

## Tone
- Preset: cinematic
- Creative direction: a trailer for infrastructure you can't subpoena
- Interpretation: wide, confident holds; big type; dramatic reveals with real air between beats. Restraint sells the claim harder than hype would — this project's own voice is architectural and matter-of-fact, so the video should feel serious even while it's showing off.

## Format: landscape — 1920x1080
## Duration: 21 seconds

## Visual identity (from the project)
- Background: #121218 (dark surface) / #FAFAFB (light surface) — use the dark palette throughout for cinematic contrast
- Accent: #5B4FE8 (light-mode primary) / #9C93FF (dark-mode primary) — indigo-violet, HOP's one distinguishing brand color
- Text: #F2F2F5 (on dark background)
- Display font: system default (Material 3 / Roboto-based) — no custom display face in the app; lean on scale and weight, not an unusual typeface
- Body font: same system family
- Strongest visual element: the indigo-violet glow against near-black, and the full-screen swipeable feed (Reels-style) as the one "product-in-use" shot worth a beat

## Share copy (draft)
Built a phone-to-phone social network with no company server, no accounts, and messages no one but you can read. Ever.

## Audio direction
- Role: cinematic support
- Music: a low, dark cinematic bed with a slow building swell — mood over melody
- Music treatment: starts near-silent under the hook, swells through the mesh-reveal scene, holds high through the encryption beat, falls away to near-silence for the punchline's spoken-word pacing, one low final hit under "Because it doesn't exist."
- Music cue guidance: to be detected at composition time (no bundled track chosen yet). Target 1 strong-cue hit at the mesh pull-back reveal (~scene 4 start) and 1 at the final "doesn't exist" line. No sequential-text beat-grid needed — the punchline's clauses should be held on their own timing, not snapped to a beat grid, since they need to be read.
- Audio-reactive treatment: subtle — the indigo glow behind the wordmark may breathe gently with the music's low end during scene 2 and the outro hold; nothing beat-synced elsewhere.
- SFX posture: sparse, motion-matched. A soft tick per tier-pill arrival, one low mechanical "click-shut" on the lock-over-message beat, no whoosh/riser library clichés.
- Audio-coupled moments: the 4 tier pills arriving in sequence (tick each), the lock snapping shut (click), the mesh pull-back (one low swell hit).
- Restraint rule: no dialogue/voice (not requested), no dense rhythmic layer, no audio that competes with the punchline's spoken-word pacing at the end — that beat needs silence around it more than it needs sound.

## Storyboard

### Scene 1 — Hook — 3s
Black frame. "THERE IS NO SERVER." slams in, huge, white, centered. Beat (~0.4s). Smaller line drops in underneath: "Because we never built one."
Sequential/interaction: yes — second line arrives after the first has fully settled, not simultaneously.
Audio intent: tension, a held breath — near silence, one low sub-bass tone under the slam.
Audio-coupled idea: a single low hit exactly on the slam of "THERE IS NO SERVER."
Music: cinematic bed, barely audible, just starting.
Transition mood: hard cut → Scene 2

### Scene 2 — Reveal — 3s
Cut to HOP's real palette: near-black background (#121218), the indigo-violet accent (#5B4FE8/#9C93FF) glowing softly behind a centered wordmark: HOP. Tagline fades in beneath: "Serverless. No accounts. Proximity-first."
Sequential/interaction: none — one clean reveal, wordmark then tagline.
Audio intent: the reveal breathing out after the hook's held tension.
Audio-coupled idea: none beyond the glow's subtle audio-reactive breathe.
Music: swell begins, still restrained.
Transition mood: dramatic wipe → Scene 3

### Scene 3 — The flow — 5s
Recreate the actual reach-tier picker from the composer screen: four pills — Locality, Town, City, Country — arrive one by one left to right, each with a soft tick. The picker settles with one selected (highlighted in the indigo accent). Immediately after, a photo card lifts and "posts" — a quick, confident motion, not a loading spinner.
Sequential/interaction: yes — 4 pills arrive in sequence (~0.5-0.6s apart, each held long enough to read the label), then the post action.
Audio intent: mechanical confidence — this is the one control the user actually touches, make it feel precise.
Audio-coupled idea: one tick per pill arrival; a slightly different, more solid sound on the post-card lift.
Music: building steadily under this scene.
Transition mood: clean wipe → Scene 4

### Scene 4 — The mesh — 6s
Two phone silhouettes appear close together with a connection line drawing between them (BLE/WiFi Direct). Camera pulls back: that connection is revealed as one edge among many in a sparse, elegant node-and-line mesh spreading across the frame (the DHT/internet-mode idea, without naming the acronym). Text overlay arrives late in the pull-back: "Phone to phone. Then anywhere." Beat, then a lock icon snaps shut over a small message-bubble glyph in the corner, with its own line: "Not even the relay can read it."
Sequential/interaction: yes — connect → pull back to reveal the wider mesh → text arrives → lock-snap beat.
Audio intent: awe on the pull-back, then a sharper, colder beat for the encryption line — two distinct emotional notes in one scene.
Audio-coupled idea: one low swell/hit exactly on the pull-back reveal; one mechanical click on the lock snapping shut.
Music: peaks here — the loudest point in the video.
Transition mood: dramatic wipe → Scene 5

### Scene 5 — Punchline / outro — 4s
Back to black. Three short lines arrive in sequence, each held on its own, unhurried: "No server to subpoena." / "No server to breach." / "No server to sell." Beat — longer than the others. Final line: "Because it doesn't exist." Wordmark HOP holds. Tagline settles beneath: "Serverless. Yours."
Sequential/interaction: yes — each of the three clauses gets its own full hold before the next arrives; this is the one place in the video where reading time matters most and must not be rushed for pace.
Audio intent: music falls away almost entirely — let the lines land in near-silence, one low final hit under "Because it doesn't exist."
Audio-coupled idea: one low hit on the final line only; the three "No server to..." lines get no individual stings, just clean silence between them.
Music: fades to near-nothing, one last low hit, then a soft tail under the final wordmark hold.
Transition mood: — (final scene)

**Music mood for this video:** cinematic
